We need a way to end an owner trust, and every way an owner stops being able to vouch should end it too; today a ship paired under a trust stays paired whatever happens to its owner.

Let the admin revoke an owner trust by its id, live or expired. It needs no password, since it only takes power away. Anyone else is refused as forbidden (403) and an unknown trust as not found (404). It records the revocation with the admin as author and the time, and tells the admin how many ships it held. A second revocation of the same trust is refused as a conflict (409), explains why and changes nothing.

When a trust is revoked, every ship whose most recent trust pairing was under it is held: its pairing confirmation goes, anything still waiting to go to it or that could not be sent to it is called back, and the content of anything it sent us is cleared. The owner is not held, and what is queued for them or sent by them stays.

This must also happen by itself whenever an owner can no longer vouch: their contact is held or deleted, their peer is unpaired or retired, or their typed code is no longer current. Each of their trusts not yet revoked, expired ones too, gets a revocation dated then, with no person as author, recording the owner's loss of eligibility as its cause, and its ships are held. Unpairing sets this off, and so does pairing a person, even a peer left unpaired with nothing held: all its contacts are held first and only the typed one is released. Running this again with nothing changed changes nothing, and each run makes known which trusts it ended, or that none did.

Pairing the owner again with their code restores them as a contact whose code was typed and is current, not held, but the trust stays ended and the ships stay held, and an assertion signed before that hold is refused (403) even under a new trust.

A held ship comes back without a code only by a trusted ship pairing under a later trust of the same owner on the same peer, granted after the revocation, with an assertion signed after it. It stays the same contact, takes the new assertion's name, loses its hold and gains a confirmation under the new trust, and nothing else is added; trying again then is refused (409) and explains why. Under an earlier trust or another owner's, refuse (409) and explain which recovery condition failed. A held contact no trust made is still refused (409) by the trusted pairing; only its own code brings it back. Typing the code of a ship held because its trust was revoked into the ordinary pairing is refused too (409), explains why, and changes nothing.

Finally, unpairing should take an option to retire the peer, for a machine we think is compromised. That records the peer, when and by whom (a second retirement keeps the first record), holds all its contacts and ends their trusts. The ordinary pairing then refuses a retired peer before comparing any code, right, wrong or empty, as a conflict (409), with the same explanation the trusted ship pairing gives for a retired peer, and writes nothing.
